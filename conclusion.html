Conclusion

As one of the most visible exemplaries of the racializing and Othering potential of waiting lines, the Brandy waiting line reflects the insidiousness of Toronto waiting lines at large, revealing the lines that the city of Toronto wishes to position itself within – lines towards the institutions of whiteness and capital, disguised as opportunities for community-building and progress. While social media users and writers are right to identify the violent transgression of this phenomenon, I hope that this article illuminates that the targets of their frustration, the ones questioned about their time and willingness to stand in long lines, are themselves exploited. Young women and girls, especially those that are racialized and Othered, face limited options to engage and belong in public collectives and are continuously weaponized for the social capital of their bodies in their aspirational ‘waiting’ to normalize and naturalize whiteness, capitalism, and gentrification. In response to the claim “People in Toronto will wait in line for anything”, I argue that women and girls in Toronto are so desperate for public engagement and collective belonging that they are willing to wait for hours for an opportunity to fulfil their desires. Unfortunately, in the empty community-building promises of trendy Toronto waiting lines, they still face a long wait ahead.
